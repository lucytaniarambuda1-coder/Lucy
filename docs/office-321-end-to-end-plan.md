# Office 321 End to End Plan

From first touch to renewal: how a contractor finds us, signs up, goes live and stays.

Prepared by Lucy Rambuda, Administrative Coordinator, Dash BPO. 10 September 2026.

## Purpose and scope

This plan maps the full Office 321 journey end to end, from the moment a contractor first hears about us to the moment they renew and refer someone else. It gives every stage a goal, an owner and a timeline, so nothing sits with "whoever gets to it".

Office 321 is an AI-powered receptionist built for contractors: roofers, plumbers, electricians, HVAC, pest control and other hands-on trades. It answers every call and chat 24/7 in English and Spanish, qualifies the customer, captures the job details, books the appointment and sends the owner a clean summary. The plan below is built around that promise: if we tell contractors "never miss another job", our own pipeline cannot drop a single lead.

**Owner names are role placeholders.** Assign a real person to each role and confirm before the plan goes live. Until then, unassigned items default to the Admin Coordinator to chase.

## Stage 1: Awareness and marketing

Goal: contractors in our target trades see Office 321 regularly, in their own language, and know exactly what we do.

- Post on Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn every week, rotating purpose: lead generation, education, community. Every post ships with an English and a Spanish version, per the brand voice guide.
- Keep the message on one idea per post, drawn from the standing proof points: answered 24/7, cheaper than a human answering service, English and Spanish included, no admin overhead, setup in days.
- Run the free tier as the standing hook: 50 calls a month at $0 gives a contractor a risk-free way in.
- Collect customer stories and missed-call numbers from live accounts (Stage 9) to feed back into content.

**Output:** a monthly content calendar, posts scheduled a week ahead, and a simple log of which posts drove enquiries.

## Stage 2: Lead capture and qualification

Goal: every enquiry, from any channel, lands in one list and gets a same-day answer. A slow reply from a company that sells fast answers kills the sale.

- Channels: website form, social DMs and comments (including "comment JOBS" style prompts), inbound calls and referrals. All of them feed one lead tracker.
- Log every lead with: name, company, trade, phone, language preference, source and date.
- Qualify with three questions: What trade are you in? Roughly how many calls do you get a week? Who answers the phone today?
- Respond within 4 business hours. If the lead came in Spanish, reply in Spanish.

**Output:** a lead tracker that is never more than one day out of date, with a status per lead: new, contacted, demo booked, quoted, won, lost.

## Stage 3: Demo and pricing

Goal: the lead hears Office 321 answer a call for their own trade, then gets pricing the same day.

- Book the demo within 3 business days of first contact. Keep it short: 20 minutes, live call demonstration in English and Spanish, then questions.
- Tailor the demo script to the lead's trade so the qualification questions sound like their real jobs.
- Send the quote the same day as the demo, with the free tier and paid plans side by side and a clear next step.
- Follow up twice on open quotes: day 3 and day 7. After that, move to a monthly nurture list rather than chasing.

**Output:** demo held, quote sent, follow-ups logged in the tracker.

## Stage 4: Sign-up and onboarding intake

Goal: turn a yes into a complete, correct setup pack within 2 days, so technical setup never stalls waiting for information.

- Send the agreement and collect the signature and billing details.
- Run the intake checklist with the customer: business name and hours, services offered and services declined, service area, pricing rules for quoting, booking calendar access, emergency and after-hours rules, greeting preferences in English and Spanish, and where summaries should be sent (text, email or both).
- Confirm the phone setup path: new number or forwarding from their existing line.
- Hand over to Onboarding with the full pack. Incomplete packs do not move to Stage 5; the gap goes back to the customer the same day.

**Output:** signed agreement, billing set up, complete intake pack handed to Onboarding.

## Stage 5: Setup and go-live

Goal: live within 5 business days of a complete intake, tested in both languages. This is where "setup in days, not weeks" gets proven true.

- Configure the receptionist from the intake pack: greeting, qualification questions, booking rules, escalation rules, summary format.
- Test before go-live: at least 5 test calls in English and 5 in Spanish, covering a standard booking, an emergency, an out-of-area caller and a price question. Fix and retest anything wrong.
- Go live with the customer on the phone: switch the forwarding together, place one live test call, confirm the summary arrives where they expect it.
- Day 2 check: review the first real calls with the customer and tune wording or rules.

**Output:** account live, test log saved, day-2 review done and any fixes applied.

## Stage 6: Live operations

Goal: every call answered, every job captured, every summary delivered. This is the product; everything else exists to feed it.

- Monitor answer rate and summary delivery daily. Any missed or dropped call on a customer account is investigated the same day.
- Watch for calls the receptionist could not handle and route them to Support for a rule fix (Stage 8).
- Track call volume per account against plan limits, so upgrades are raised with the customer before overage surprises them.

**Output:** a daily operations check, logged, with exceptions raised the same day.

## Stage 7: Billing and account admin

Goal: invoices go out on time, match the plan and usage, and payment issues get handled before they become cancellations.

- Run the monthly billing cycle: verify usage per account, issue invoices, reconcile payments.
- Contact any customer approaching their plan limit before the invoice lands, with the upgrade option in plain terms.
- Chase failed payments within 2 business days, by phone first. A billing call is also a check-in call.
- Keep account records current: contact person, plan, add-ons, start date, renewal date.

**Output:** invoices out on the same date every month, an aged-debt list that stays short, and clean account records.

## Stage 8: Support and quality

Goal: problems fixed fast and call quality checked on a schedule, not only when someone complains.

- One support inbox and number, answered within 1 business day. Every ticket logged with account, issue, fix and time to resolve.
- Weekly quality review: sample recent calls per account, in both languages, against a short checklist: right greeting, right questions, correct booking, accurate summary.
- Feed recurring issues back into setup templates so new accounts do not inherit old problems.

**Output:** ticket log with resolution times, weekly quality review notes, and template updates where needed.

## Stage 9: Retention, reviews and referrals

Goal: customers stay because they can see the value in jobs and money, and the happiest ones feed Stages 1 and 2.

- Check-in calls at 30, 90 and 180 days, then every 6 months. Bring their numbers: calls answered, after-hours calls captured, Spanish calls captured, jobs booked.
- At the 90-day check-in, ask the happy customers for a review and a referral. Referrals go straight into the lead tracker with the referrer noted.
- Ask permission to use their story and numbers in marketing, feeding Stage 1 with real proof.
- Any customer showing churn signals (falling usage, unanswered check-ins, billing issues) gets a call from the account owner within a week.

**Output:** check-in log, review and referral count per month, and a churn-risk list reviewed weekly.

## Operating rhythm

|---|---|

## Risks and watch-outs

- **Slow lead response.** The most damaging failure, because it contradicts the pitch. The 4-hour rule is the one target that never flexes.
- **Incomplete intake packs.** The main cause of slow go-lives. The Stage 4 gate (no handover on an incomplete pack) exists to stop this.
- **Spanish quality drift.** Bilingual coverage is the differentiator, so Spanish calls get sampled in every weekly quality review, not occasionally.
- **Overage surprises.** A customer's first sign of a usage limit should be a friendly call from us, never a bigger invoice.
- **Single-person dependencies.** Every stage needs a named backup, so a sick day does not stall the pipeline.
